03 · the full verdict

Right sometimes, wrong sometimes — and we can tell which

All 30 dreams, plotted as imagined block displacement against the real displacement the simulator produced. Points on the dashed line mean the model was right. Shape and colour encode the verifier's classification.

The line is where imagination equals reality. Supported dreams cluster on it; the do-nothing dreams sit far off it (the model mispredicts coasting). Not shown: 5 random-action dreams the model sent flying to 7,500–17,000 m — raw imagination diverges off any axis, which is exactly why verification is not optional.

05 · learning from dreams

Does imagination make the model better?

The real test. Given a fixed budget of 30 real experiments, does choosing them with imagination beat choosing at random? Each bar retrains the model on base data plus 450 chosen transitions and measures held-out error (lower is better). The line is the no-new-data baseline; a bar only counts if it drops below it.

adopted (beat baseline) rolled back (worse) base, no new data
What it shows. When exploratory dreams roam outside the deployment distribution (left panel), every strategy hurts — chasing the "most surprising" dream optimises for the wrong physics. In-distribution (right panel), the oracle — targeting the experiments where the model is genuinely most wrong — beats both base and random. That proves the potential is real, but today's pre-verification signals (uncertainty, coverage) can't find those experiments yet. And training on the model's own imagined transitions degrades it: raw imagination must be verified before it can teach. Rollback rejected 9 of 10 updates.

How this was made. Every frame is a real MuJoCo replay; every number is measured on held-out data, never on the model's own predictions. Imagined trajectories are plotted as numbers, never rendered as if they were real.

Honest limits. One task, a tiny dataset, 5 seeds, and high run-to-run variance. The oracle's win is about one standard deviation — consistent across seeds but modest. This is a working mechanism with an open selection problem, not a solved system.
